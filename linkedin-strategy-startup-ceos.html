A 4-pillar approach, not a promise

Startup CEOs are told to post, and then told that posting will somehow become the company. The useful version is quieter. This 4-pillar approach is how we decide what is worth saying on LinkedIn. Use it whether or not you hire anyone. Thought leadership, in this framework, is what the pillars produce when the point of view is real. Company growth is not a pillar. It is what a clearer company is allowed to earn.

The four pillars are stance, evidence from the work, one reader, and rhythm. None of them is a content theme. Themes rotate. Pillars stay.

Pillar 1. Stance

Say what the company believes, in language a customer would recognise. A stance is specific enough that a rival could disagree. "We care about customers" is not a stance. "We would rather lose a deal than ship this kind of compromise" can be one, if it is true and you behave that way.

The CEO should be able to say the stance without notes. If it only works as a caption under a photograph, it is not ready for a feed that serious people read.

Pillar 2. Evidence from the work

Readers trust operating detail more than a manifesto. What changed in the product, the sale, the hire, or the mistake. Keep it inside what you are willing to share. Do not borrow a statistic to sound larger than the company is.

Evidence is also how investors and candidates do their own diligence. They are not owed your board pack. They are quick to notice when every post is a claim and none of them is a scene from the work. Give them the scene.

Pillar 3. One reader

Each post has one primary reader: a buyer, a candidate, a peer, or the team. Write to that person. Others may read over their shoulder. If you write to everyone, you write the bland middle, and the people you wanted will not feel addressed.

This is the same rule as executive communications inside the company. One message, one reader at a time. The public version is shorter. It is not looser.

Pillar 4. Rhythm and reply

Publishing is half of the strategy. The other half is the conversation under the post and in the messages that follow. A CEO who never replies is broadcasting. Plan a rhythm you can keep, and a habit of answering the serious comments. A rhythm you abandon in week three teaches the market that the stance was a campaign.

Strategic LinkedIn content is this loop, repeated: stance, evidence, one reader, reply. Do it long enough that the record is coherent. People who should know you, including investors, can then tell what you are building. Treat that as a consequence. Do not treat a framework as a warrant for a revenue figure. This page refuses that shortcut on purpose.
