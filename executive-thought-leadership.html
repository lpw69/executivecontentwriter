A useful definition

Executive thought leadership is a point of view the market can use. It is not a slogan, and it is not a diary of the working week. If a reader in your category can act differently, or argue with you properly, the piece has done its job.

Industry authority is the same test, seen from outside. People grant it when your public record matches your work. They do not grant it because a page says you are a leader. Positioning is the choice of what to be known for. It is a decision, made before the draft, about the problem you are willing to be quoted on for a year.

Point of view

A point of view needs a subject, a stance, and a boundary. The subject is the problem you actually see. The stance is what you believe should happen. The boundary is what you will not claim.

Leaders often skip the boundary. That is how thought leadership becomes advertising. Say what you will not promise. The readers who are paying attention trust a limit faster than they trust a boast. A stance a rival could not disagree with is not a stance. It is a caption.

Formats that can carry it

A short post can hold a stance if the sentence is sharp. A longer note can hold the evidence. A talk should be the same idea, said to a room, not a new personality for the afternoon.

Cadence

Cadence is a promise to your own thinking. It is not a promise to a feed. Once a week, with a real point, is more useful than a daily post that repeats a slogan. The test is whether the executive still recognises the argument after a month.

Clients live within two weeks of a start, so the cadence begins while the voice is still being learned. That is uncomfortable and correct. Waiting for a perfect voice is how the public record stays empty. The 21-day get-out clause is there if the argument and the person do not match.

What to leave out

Leave out trophies you would not mention in a serious meeting. Leave out other people's numbers. Leave out a campaign about a topic you do not work on. More than a billion views across socials to date is the only reach figure this site uses. It describes our work. It is not a target we paint onto your name.
