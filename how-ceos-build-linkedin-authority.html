How CEOs build authority on LinkedIn

Authority is a record

A CEO builds authority on LinkedIn the same way they build it anywhere else: by being recognisable on a problem they actually work on. The feed is the archive. People grant attention when the archive matches the work.

Executive communications asks a prior question. Does the public line agree with the line inside the company? If it does not, posting will not make the leader sound like a person others should quote. Fix the message. Then publish it where the readers already are. Often that place is LinkedIn. The communications guide is the discipline. This page is the public version for a chief executive.

The sentence anyone could sign

The usual failure is a post a peer could have published without changing a word. It has the right topic and none of the decision. Readers learn nothing they could not have guessed.

A house pattern produces this. So does a list of topics with no stance. So does a story that flatters the company and never names a constraint. The repair is not a stranger topic. It is the same topic, told as a choice: what you did, what you refused, and why a sensible person might have chosen the other way.

The thought leadership guide puts it as subject, stance, and boundary. Authority is what that looks like after enough weeks that a reader can anticipate your view, and can tell when you have changed it on purpose.

What to put in the record

Use the four moves in the startup CEO framework.

  • A stance a rival could disagree with, if it is true.
  • Evidence from the work you are willing to share. A scene, not a borrowed number.
  • One reader. The post is for a buyer, a candidate, a peer, or the team. Not for an abstract audience called leaders.
  • A rhythm you can keep, including replies. Authority that never answers is a notice on a wall. The conversation under the post is part of the record.

None of this requires a special format. It requires that the chief executive still recognises the argument when they read it back.

Where a writer helps

An executive writer helps when the CEO has the judgement and does not have the draft. The material is how you explain the decision to someone who was not in the room. Not a list of subjects that happen to be current. Not a house manner that sands the limit off the sentence.

Used this way, the writer is doing what a good in-house communications partner does. Holding the line steady, cutting the sentence that overclaims, and keeping a note of the arguments that are actually yours. The byline stays the CEO's because the decision stayed the CEO's.

What authority is not

It is not a follower count. It is not a post that travels to people who did not need it. It is not a slogan repeated until it looks like a position. We will not put a number on the commercial result of a clearer public record. Clarity is the work. What a serious reader does next is theirs.

Questions

Common questions

How does a CEO become known for a point of view?

By publishing the same standard of decision, with evidence, long enough that the record is coherent.

Can a writer create authority for me?

No. A writer can keep your line clear and regular. The view has to be yours.

How is this different from posting often?

Frequency without a stance is noise on a schedule. Rhythm matters only after the sentence is one you would sign.
