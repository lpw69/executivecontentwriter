LinkedIn content writer or social media manager

Two jobs

A social media manager and a LinkedIn content writer can both touch the same profile. They are not substitutes. One is accountable for activity across networks. The other is accountable for whether a leader's public sentence is true, specific, and in that leader's judgement.

Founders blur them because both can be described as handling LinkedIn. The blur shows up as a feed that looks busy and says nothing the company would repeat inside the building. That is an executive communications failure, not a scheduling failure. The communications guide is the standard the sentence has to meet before anyone argues about tools.

What a social manager is for

Hire a social media manager when the company needs a maintained presence in several places, and someone to keep the calendar, the assets, and the everyday replies moving. That role is real. It is the right hire when LinkedIn is one channel among others and the voice is already settled.

It is a poor hire when you expect that person to supply the chief executive's point of view. A generalist can adapt a line that already exists. They should not be asked to decide what the leader believes.

What an executive writer is for

Hire an executive content writer when the leader is the voice and the draft is what slips. The work is in-house in spirit even when the person is external. Close to the message, clear about limits, nothing public without approval.

On LinkedIn, that means a note a serious reader can use or argue with. The same tests as the rest of this site: a stance a rival could disagree with, evidence from the work, and one reader at a time. The framework is the operating version. The executive writer page is the role when you look past a single network.

We do not describe the job as a method for collecting attention. Attention from the wrong room is not the aim. A sentence the right person can answer is closer to the work. If the brief you were handed talks only about volume across platforms, you are still looking at the other job.

How to tell from the brief

Read the brief you are about to send, or the proposal in front of you.

If it leads with channels, asset sizes, and a posting quota, it is a social management brief.

If it leads with the message, the reader, the claims you will not make, and who approves, it is an executive writing brief.

A company can need both, once it is large enough to hold both. Do not ask one person to pretend they are doing the second job while they were employed to do the first. If you are hiring the writer, use the hiring guide. If you are still deciding whether to write it yourself, use that comparison.

The mistake to avoid

The mistake is to treat the leader's name as a channel that needs filling. Executive content writing starts from the other end. The sentence first, then the few places it deserves. LinkedIn is often one of those places for a founder, because customers, candidates and peers already read it. That does not earn the sentence a lower standard.

Questions

Common questions

Can a social media manager write a CEO's LinkedIn?

They can publish on the account. Whether they should hold the line depends on the job you hired. Often it is not that job.

Do I have to choose one role forever?

No. Some companies need both. Do not collapse them into one title and hope the writing takes care of itself.
