A content writer for executives

A content writer for executives works on thought leadership the leader will sign, communications the team can repeat, and public posts that match what the company is actually doing. The service is not a content engine. It is a way of keeping C-suite writing accurate.

Volume is the usual failure. Teams add formats until nobody can say what the company believes. The corrective is a smaller set of messages, written properly. This page is about that service. The executive writer page is about the role. The communications guide is about the discipline underneath both.

The audiences in the room

Most weak executive content speaks to "the industry". Useful writing picks one reader.

  • Customers, who want to know whether you understand their constraint.
  • Candidates, who are deciding what it would feel like to work with you.
  • The company, who need a line they can use without translating it.
  • Investors and partners, who are listening for judgement under pressure.

One post can be seen by all four. It should still be written for one of them. If you cannot name the reader, you are not ready to draft.

A writing system that stays small

We do not sell a stack of named packages. The public offer is founder-led LinkedIn and X ghostwriting from $2,500 a month. Around that, the system is the same whatever the format.

  1. Decide the point. If you cannot say it in one sentence, the draft is early.
  2. Add evidence from the work, not a borrowed statistic.
  3. Remove any line the executive would not say out loud.
  4. Publish, then read the replies. The conversation is part of the communication.

That system is deliberately boring. Executive content writing fails when it tries to be a performance. It works when a colleague can read the draft and still hear the person whose name is on it.

What good looks like

Good executive content is specific, calm, and willing to exclude. It does not borrow urgency from a trend. It does not flatter the reader. It can survive being quoted back in a meeting.

More than a billion views across socials to date sits behind this practice. We still do not name clients. A logo row is not a standard you can reuse. The standard is on the page: one reader, one point, evidence you actually have, and a price you can read before you talk to us. That price is explained on the pricing page.
