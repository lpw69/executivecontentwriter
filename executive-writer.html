The role

Business leaders already have opinions. They rarely have a clear hour, a structure, and someone who will say a line is weak. An executive writer supplies those three. The role sits beside the leader, not in front of the brand. When there is a byline, it is the executive's. The writer's job is to make that byline safe to use.

This is different from a campaign copywriter, who is hired to make an offer move. An executive writer is hired so that the person running the company can speak in public without a weekly scramble, and without sounding like a brochure. LinkedIn, a short note to the company, and a point of view in a newsletter can all come from the same judgement. They should.

Voice, without theatre

Voice is not a list of adjectives in a deck. It is the words someone actually reaches for, the jokes they will not make, and the claims they will not sign. A good executive writer listens for those limits and writes inside them.

If a draft could have been published by any founder in the category, it is not finished. Specificity is the test. So is refusal. The sentences you will not say are part of the voice, and a writer who never asks about them is guessing.

What gets written

LinkedIn and X are the regular public surfaces, and they are the work we price in public: founder-led ghostwriting from $2,500 a month. They are not the whole of executive communication. A leadership note, a short talk, or a difficult explanation to customers may matter more in a given month.

Newsletters and thought leadership belong in the same practice when they carry the same point of view. We do not publish a separate fee for each format. A wider brief is scoped on a call, so this page does not pretend there is a catalogue. The thought leadership guide explains the standard those longer pieces have to meet.

A working rhythm

The first fortnight is for fit. Clients live within two weeks. You should hear your own judgement in the draft, not a borrowed tone. If you do not, the 21-day get-out clause exists so both sides can stop without a performance.

After that, the rhythm is plain. A point of view, a piece of evidence from the work, and a reason for the right reader to care. Volume without those three is noise with a calendar attached. If you are deciding whether to hire, the hiring guide is the checklist. If you want the number first, it is on the pricing page.
