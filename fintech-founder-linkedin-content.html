The line you are responsible for

A fintech founder does not have a spare public voice. Customers, candidates, partners and the people who check claims are all reading the same name. Executive communications is the discipline of keeping that name consistent with what the company actually does.

LinkedIn is one place the line appears. It is not a separate channel with a looser standard. A post is still the founder speaking. If a sentence would not survive a serious internal meeting, it should not be published under that name.

This page is a writing guide for that problem. It does not tour product categories, and it does not describe a sales programme. The question is narrower. What is this leader allowed to say, in their own words, about the work.

Readers who are not one audience

Write one message. Then choose which reader this particular note is for. A buyer who must trust the firm, a candidate deciding whether the company is serious, and a colleague who will notice a loose claim are not the same reader. One note that tries to please all three becomes a brochure.

The test is whether someone inside the company can repeat the point without correcting it. If the public line and the internal line disagree, repair the message before you argue about a channel. The communications guide is the longer version of that rule. It applies with more force when the company handles other people's money or other people's risk.

What a public sentence may hold

A useful post holds a decision the founder will stand behind. What you chose, what you refused, and the constraint that made the choice real. Readers in financial services hear a promise quickly. Do not give them one by accident.

Leave out language that sounds like a forecast, a return, or a guarantee of how a product will behave. Leave out a feature list with the verbs softened until it looks like an essay. Leave out an announcement that teaches the reader nothing about how you decide. The thought leadership guide is the test: a point of view has a subject, a stance, and a boundary. In a regulated firm the boundary is the part that keeps the sentence honest.

You do not need a catalogue of specialisms to apply this. The writing problem is the same across the work: say what you decided, say what you will not claim, and stop.

Briefing a writer the way you would brief an in-house lead

An executive content writer, used properly, works as a careful in-house communications partner would. They sit close to the message. They do not invent a position the founder has not taken. They do not stand in for the people inside the firm whose job is to check a claim. They are not counsel, and they should not pretend to be.

A workable brief has four parts.

  • The message. The sentence that has to survive a retelling, in language you would use out loud.
  • The limit. The words, and the kinds of claim, the company will not make. A writer cannot guess the line your own reviewers care about. Write it down.
  • The evidence you are willing to share. A scene from the work, not a borrowed figure. If a number cannot be said in a customer meeting, it cannot be said in a post.
  • The reader for this stretch of writing. One primary reader. Others may look over their shoulder.

The startup CEO framework is the same shape: stance, evidence from the work, one reader, and rhythm. A fintech founder does not need a different theory of writing. They need a stricter boundary around claims, and a writer who will keep it.

Sign-off

Nothing goes out that you would not say in a room with your name on the door. You approve the line. If a sentence needs a second reader inside the company, that reader sees it before it is public. Build that pause into the week. A writer who treats the pause as a nuisance is the wrong writer for this work.

The notes on how you speak, which claims are off limits, and which arguments are actually yours should stay with the company. If the engagement ends, that record should still be yours. An in-house communications lead would leave it in the firm's files. An external writer working in that style should do the same.
